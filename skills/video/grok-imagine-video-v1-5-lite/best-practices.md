# Grok Imagine Video 1.5 Lite — Best Practices

Depth on prompt construction. The hard rules live in `SKILL.md`.

---

## 1. Write for the upsampler and the first frame

Two documented pipeline facts shape every Lite prompt:

1. **An upsampler LLM rewrites the prompt** before generation (xAI usage schema: *"Prompt text tokens consumed
   by the prompt-rewriting (upsampler) LLM"*). Every element you leave unstated — light direction, camera
   behavior, what the room sounds like — is chosen by the rewrite, and its choices are generic.
2. **Text-to-video renders a first frame, then animates it** (*"text-to-image then image-to-video under the
   hood"*). The opening composition is effectively an image prompt.

So: **open with the frame** (shot size, angle, subject placement, setting, light), **then the motion**, **then
the sound**. One specific paragraph, roughly 50–130 words. A 15-word prompt does not give a "focused" Lite
clip — it gives an upsampled clip you didn't specify.

## 2. Keep it shot-sized

xAI recommends Lite *"for simple videos."* Build each prompt around:

- **one subject** (or two at most, for a dialogue two-shot),
- **one dominant action**, told in order with a clear end state,
- **one camera behavior**, with where it lands,
- **one sound bed** plus one or two synced effects.

Establishing shot + chase + reveal + dialogue in 8 seconds will compress or drop beats. Generate separate
clips and cut them together.

## 3. Describe visible motion

| ❌ Abstract | ✅ Physical |
|---|---|
| "the city feels alive" | "pedestrians cross in layered streams, tram cables sway, neon slides across wet pavement" |
| "she is nervous" | "she twists her ring twice and glances at the door" |
| "dynamic product shot" | "the bottle rotates a quarter turn as a highlight travels down the glass edge" |
| "epic weather" | "rain sheets sideways across the frame, puddles shiver with each gust" |

Sequence beats with plain connectives — "first… then… as it settles…" — rather than packing simultaneous
actions into one clause. Community guides suggest broad timecodes like `(0–3s)` for multi-beat clips; xAI
does not document them for Lite, so treat them as untested and prefer ordered prose (unverified — confirm on
your platform).

## 4. Camera language

No camera-control field exists. Write it:

- **Static:** "locked off on a tripod", "fixed frame"
- **Push / pull:** "slow dolly in to a close-up", "pulls back to reveal the room"
- **Lateral:** "tracks left alongside her at waist height"
- **Orbit:** "a slow 30-degree orbit clockwise around the machine"
- **Vertical:** "cranes up over the rooftops"
- **Texture:** "handheld with slight drift", "stabilized gimbal"
- **Lens feel:** "wide-angle with close foreground", "compressed telephoto", "shallow depth of field"

One primary move per clip. "Locked off" and "fast orbit" in the same prompt is a contradiction the upsampler
will resolve for you. Terms circulating for Grok such as `camera switch` or `fixed lens` / `unfixed lens` have
no first-party basis — don't build prompts on them.

## 5. Audio — prompt it like picture

Audio is generated by default and arrives synced to the picture. Specify each layer, or the model invents it:

- **Ambient bed:** room tone, rain on glass, distant traffic, wind through grass
- **Synced effects (1–2):** tied to visible events — "a cup set down", "the latch clicks"
- **Music:** genre + intensity + when it enters, or "no music"
- **Dialogue:** see §6
- **Acoustics:** "dry, close", "long reverb in a stone hall"

If the clip should be silent, switch audio off in settings rather than describing silence.

## 6. Dialogue and lip-sync

Lite generates **lip-synced speech** natively (xAI: *"`grok-imagine-video-1.5-lite` does text-to-video and
image-to-video with lip-synced speech"*). xAI's own dialogue examples write lines as `Sam: "I took the job in
Berlin."` and `She says: "Welcome to my dream living room."` (shown on full 1.5).

- **Name or describe the speaker** immediately before the quoted line, and make them visible in frame.
- **Short lines.** One line per speaker per clip; a few seconds of speech in an 8-second shot. Long speeches
  crowd the action and drift out of sync.
- **Two speakers is the practical ceiling.** Place them clearly ("on the left… on the right…") so the right
  mouth moves.
- **Delivery in words:** "quietly, without looking up", "clipped and flat". Lite has no voice selection —
  timbre comes from the description ("a low, gravelly voice").
- **No voice tokens.** `<AUDIO_0>` is a full-1.5 feature; on Lite it is literal text.
- If the speaker's voice must match across many shots, route that character to full 1.5 (preset or uploaded
  voice references) or a dedicated voice model.

## 7. Image-to-video

The `image` becomes frame 1. The prompt is optional — but almost always worth writing.

- **Describe only change:** motion, camera behavior, light shift, sound.
- **Name the 2–3 things that must hold:** "the label text stays exact", "her face and hair unchanged". xAI's
  own guidance is to *"say what must stay the same."*
- **Don't re-describe the image.** Contradicting it ("a red coat" over a blue coat) invites a morph.
- **Aspect ratio comes from the image.** Crop or pad the source to the delivery ratio before you submit.
- Keep motion modest when the frame is precious; big moves reveal regions the image never showed.

## 8. Exclusions and stability

There is **no negative-prompt field**, and whether in-prompt negation works is not documented by xAI.

- Prefer positive description: "an empty platform, benches bare" over "no people".
- **Stability statements are first-party style** ("label text stays exact", "keep the decals unchanged") —
  use them freely.
- Short audio/text exclusions ("no music", "no subtitles or on-screen text") are widely used by the community
  for dialogue scenes; treat them as helpful but untested (unverified — confirm on your platform).

## 9. Resolution, cost and the 1080p upscale

| Tier | Use it for | Lite price |
|---|---|---|
| 480p | Composition and motion checks | $0.02/s |
| 720p | Judging audio, lip-sync, finished web delivery | $0.03/s |
| 1080p | Chosen finals only — it is the 720p render upscaled | $0.14/s |

Upscaling adds pixels, not detail: fine texture, small text and distant faces are decided at 720p. If a shot
needs native 1080p detail, that is a reason to use full 1.5 ($0.25/s at 1080p).

## 10. Mistakes and fixes

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Generic look despite a clear idea | Short prompt; upsampler filled gaps | Specify frame, camera, light and sound |
| Beats compressed or dropped | Too many actions for one clip | One action per clip; split the rest |
| Wrong person's mouth moves | Speakers not placed or named | Name each speaker, place them left/right, one line each |
| Lines garbled or out of sync | Lines too long for the clip | Shorter lines, fewer of them |
| Unwanted score under dialogue | Audio layer left open | Say "no music" or describe the bed fully |
| Image stretched or ignored ratio | `aspect_ratio` set on image-to-video | Crop/pad the source image instead |
| Subject morphs in image-to-video | Prompt re-describes the image differently | Describe change only; state what holds |
| Fine detail mushy at 1080p | Lite 1080p is upscaled | Judge at 720p; use full 1.5 for native 1080p |
| Request rejected | Full-1.5 fields (`reference_images`, `last_frame`, `keyframes`, `reference_audios`) on Lite | Remove them or switch to full 1.5 |
| Can't recreate a good take | No seed | Save prompt, source image, settings and output |
| Clip came out 6 s, not 8 | Wrapper default | Set `duration` explicitly |
