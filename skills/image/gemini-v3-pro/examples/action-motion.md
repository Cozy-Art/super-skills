# Action & Motion Examples - Gemini 3 Pro

This file contains examples for creating dynamic scenes with implied motion, action, and kinetic energy.

## Example 1: Athlete in Motion - Basketball Jump Shot

### Scenario
Sports action for athletic brand content.

### Prompt
```
Create a dynamic sports photograph of a basketball player mid-jump shot. The athlete 
is a young man in his early 20s with athletic build, wearing a sleeveless white jersey 
and black shorts. Captured at the apex of his jump, arms fully extended upward releasing 
the ball, legs bent slightly, face focused intently on the basket above. His body is 
perfectly aligned in form, showing athletic grace. The background is an outdoor court at 
golden hour, slightly blurred to emphasize the subject. Warm sunset light comes from behind 
and to the left, creating dramatic rim lighting that highlights the contours of his arms 
and face. Shot with 70-200mm lens at f/2.8 for frozen motion but shallow background. 
Fast shutter speed freezing the action perfectly sharp. 3:4 portrait orientation. The mood 
is intense, athletic, aspirational. Sports photography aesthetic with high contrast and 
rich color saturation.
```

### Configuration Notes
- **Resolution:** 4K (capture muscle definition and motion detail)
- **Aspect Ratio:** 3:4 (standard sports portrait)
- **Key Element:** "Frozen motion" at peak action moment

## Motion Techniques Reference

### Frozen Motion (Fast Shutter)
```
Ultra-fast shutter speed (1/1000s - 1/8000s) freezing action at peak moment. Use for: 
sports at apex of jump, water splashes, flying fabric, athletes in motion. Sharp throughout 
with captured details impossible to see with naked eye.
```

### Motion Blur (Slow Shutter)
```
Slower shutter speed (1/15s - 1/60s) allowing selective blur. Subject can be sharp with 
blurred background (panning) or intentionally blurred subject showing motion. Use for: 
dance movement with ethereal quality, speed and energy suggestions, artistic flow.
```

### Panning Motion
```
Camera follows moving subject at matched speed. Subject stays sharp while background blurs 
horizontally. Shutter speed 1/30s - 1/125s depending on subject speed. Use for: cycling, 
running, cars, conveying speed while maintaining subject clarity.
```

### Implied Motion
```
Composition and pose suggest movement about to happen or just completed. No actual motion 
blur but dynamic positioning. Use for: pre-action moments, athletic ready positions, 
anticipatory poses.
```

## Action Composition Guidelines

### Peak Action Moment
Capture the decisive moment—highest point of jump, moment of contact, apex of movement. This creates maximum visual impact.

### Leading Space
Leave space in the frame in the direction of movement. Subject running right should have more space on right side of frame.

### Dynamic Angles
Low angles make subjects appear more powerful. High angles show scale and relationship to environment. Dutch angles (tilted) add instability and energy.

### Motion Lines
Use leading lines in environment to enhance direction of motion—roads, paths, architectural elements that align with movement.

### Sequential Suggestion
Sometimes showing before/after or multiple phases of motion in single composition can tell a more complete action story.

## Technical Settings for Motion

| Action Type | Shutter Speed | Aperture | Focus Mode |
|-------------|---------------|----------|------------|
| Frozen sports action | 1/1000s - 1/4000s | f/2.8 - f/5.6 | Continuous AF |
| Panning motion | 1/30s - 1/125s | f/5.6 - f/8 | Continuous AF |
| Dance/fabric motion | 1/500s - 1/2000s | f/2.8 - f/4 | Single AF |
| Water splash | 1/2000s - 1/8000s | f/8 - f/11 | Single AF |
| Running/cycling pan | 1/60s - 1/250s | f/4 - f/8 | Continuous AF |
